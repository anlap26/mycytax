Buying property in Cyprus: taxes and fees checklist 2026

The published taxes on a purchase, and the ones that appear later if you let or sell. Not a fee for this site, and not an assessment of your contract.

Figures on this site are taken from the official and primary sources linked on each page and describe the position as published for 2026. They are general information, not legal, tax or investment advice, and not a quote from the Land Registry or the Tax Department. Rules, reliefs and local fees change, and a transaction can fall outside the ordinary case. Check the linked source, and take advice on your own facts, before you rely on a result.

Transfer fees or VAT, not usually both

Gov.cy states that transfer fees are not charged when the transfer is a transaction subject to VAT, if you produce the VAT Department certificate. Where VAT is not charged, the legislation provides a 50% exemption from transfer fees. The 50% is refused where the seller acquired the property in a forced sale under Parts VI and VIA of the Transfer and Mortgage Law 9/1965. The Department of Lands and Surveys scale, before that 50%, is 3% from €0.01 to €85,000, 5% from €85,001 to €170,000, and 8% above €170,000. The bands are slices. On an accepted value of €200,000 with one buyer and no VAT, the full scale is €9,200 and half is €4,600. A contract not deposited within six months can increase the transfer fee by 10% when title is transferred, according to the Department’s leaflet.

VAT at 19%, or 5% on part of a primary home

The standard rate is 19% of the price before VAT. The 5% rate is for a primary and permanent residence, on the first 130 square metres and up to €350,000, and only if the whole buildable area is not over 190 square metres and the whole value is not over €475,000. Between those inner and outer limits only the qualifying portion is at 5%. Over either outer limit the reduced rate is not available. A holiday home, or a flat you will let from the start, is budgeted at 19%. The window for the transitional reduced-rate application ran to 15 June 2026. This checklist does not treat that route as still open.

Stamp duty

Law 239(I)/2025 repealed the stamp duty laws from 1 January 2026. A contract drawn up and signed by at least one party on or after that date is not stamped: the duty is €0. A contract that a party signed on or before 31 December 2025 is still on the old scale, capped at €20,000 on one document. The Ministry of Interior checklist has not always been rewritten for the repeal. Say so when you file.

If you later sell: capital gains tax at 20%

Capital gains tax is 20% of the gain on disposal of immovable property in Cyprus. The Tax Department reduces the gain for inflation using the official retail price index. This checklist does not store that index: take the ratio from the Department’s table. For an individual, the consolidated Capital Gains Tax Law states a lifetime exemption of €30,000, €50,000 where a farmer sells agricultural land, and €150,000 on a qualifying main residence used by the owner for at least five years (ten years if it is not the first such disposal) on land of up to 1.5 decares. A person does not get both the general exemption and the residence exemption. Companies do not get these lifetime exemptions.

If you let

A 20% deduction from gross rent is the statutory income-tax deduction. From 1 January 2026 the bands for an individual are nil on the first €22,000, 20% to €32,000, 25% to €42,000, 30% to €72,000 and 35% above that. Special Defence Contribution on rent is nil for the 2026 tax year, including for domiciled residents. GHS on rental income is 2.65% of the gross rent. An annual ceiling can apply and is not reproduced here.

Costs that are not a tax rate

Legal fees, the Land Registry search, the fee to deposit the contract, utility deposits and municipal charges are not on a single published percentage. Ask for them in writing. Do not add a national annual property tax: that state tax is not charged for tax years from 2017.
