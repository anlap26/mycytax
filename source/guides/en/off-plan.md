---
title: Buying off-plan from a Cyprus developer
description: The permits, the mother title, the mortgage waiver and the contract deposit to check before you pay stage by stage.
---

Buying off-plan means paying for a unit that does not yet have its own title deed, and often does not yet exist as a finished flat. The protection is not a brochure. It is a planning permit, a building permit, a clear search of the land the building stands on, a contract the Land Registry will accept, and a payment schedule that matches work you can see. Cyprus does not operate a general statutory escrow that holds the whole price until the deed is issued. Anyone who tells you the law “parks the money safely” is describing a contractual account, if one has been agreed, not a rule of the land law.

## The land and the permits

The building stands on a registered plot. Ask for the title number and a recent search certificate from the Department of Lands and Surveys. The certificate should show the registered owner. At least one seller named in your contract has to be that owner, or the Land Registry can refuse to deposit the contract under the Specific Performance Law. If the plot is in the name of a different company from the one selling to you, stop until the chain is explained in writing.

The search also shows mortgages and memos. A developer mortgage on the whole plot is common. It is acceptable only with a written bank waiver that releases your unit as the price is paid, and a follow-up search that shows the waiver was filed. A sentence in the contract that the developer “will obtain” a waiver is a promise. The filed waiver is the fact.

Ask for the planning permit and the building permit, and check that the unit you are buying is within them. A planning application that has been “submitted” is not a permit. The VAT transitional rule, which looked at whether a planning application was in by 31 October 2023, is a tax rule. It is not evidence that the building is lawful. The window for filing the reduced-VAT application under that transitional rule ended on 15 June 2026. Do not pay a reservation on the strength of a 5 per cent rate unless the Tax Department’s conditions are met for you, not merely for the project.

## The company

The seller should be a company you can find at the Registrar of Companies: the registered office, the directors, and whether it is filing accounts. A brand name on a hoarding is not the contracting party. Read the name on the draft contract against the name on the title. If they differ, ask why, in writing, before you pay a reservation fee. Reservation fees have a habit of being described as non-refundable. If the reservation is not the contract, know what you lose if you walk away, and what you lose if the developer walks away.

## The contract

It needs the parties, a description that ties the unit to the plan, the area, the parking and the store if they are included, the price, the VAT treatment, the dates of payment, and the specification. It needs a date for delivery, and a consequence if that date is missed, that you are actually willing to use. A long list of extensions “for any cause” can make the date meaningless. The contract must be signed by all parties if you want to deposit it.

Deposit it at the district lands office within six months. The Department’s leaflet says a deposit stops a later contract for the same unit being accepted, preserves a court application for specific performance, and avoids a 10 per cent uplift in transfer fees. The statutory conditions are in Law 81(I)/2011. There is no advantage in waiting for the roof to be on. The charge you want is the earliest one you can get.

Stage payments should follow stages you can verify: foundations, frame, brickwork, completion. Paying 80 per cent on signature, against a hole in the ground and a mortgage you have not seen released, is a risk the statute does not soften. If a stakeholder account is offered, read who can sign a release and what certificate they need. If it is not offered, price that into the decision. Do not invent an escrow the contract does not contain.

## VAT and the price you think you are paying

Developer sales of new buildings are the usual VAT case. Budget 19 per cent unless you personally qualify for 5 per cent on a primary residence within 130 square metres and €350,000, inside a property that is not over 190 square metres or €475,000. The developer’s sales office does not grant the 5 per cent rate. The Tax Department does, on an application. If you do not qualify, the cheap headline price is a price before 19 per cent. Transfer fees should not also be charged on that VAT transfer, if the certificate is produced. Confirm which figure the payment schedule uses, so you are not paying 19 per cent on top of a price that was already said to include it.

## Snagging and the deed that comes later

Completion in the contract and completion for a separate title are different events. You can be handed keys, and still wait years for the division of the title. Budget for that wait. Your deposited contract is what you hold in the meantime. Before you accept the keys, list defects against the specification. A snagging list sent the same week is more useful than a list sent after the next buyer’s works have covered the evidence.

When the separate title is issued, the transfer still needs the capital-gains clearance on the developer’s side, your acquisition permission if you are a non-EU buyer, and the VAT certificate. None of those documents is produced by the fact that you have been living there.

## If it goes wrong

A deposited contract is the basis of a claim for an order that the unit be registered in your name, or for compensation. It is not a quick administrative form. Insolvency of the developer makes the existing mortgage and the order of charges the whole story. This is why the search and the waiver come before the first large payment, not after the second.

## FAQ

### Does Cyprus law hold my payments in escrow until the deed is ready?

There is no general rule that does that. Any safety comes from the contract, the bank waiver and the deposited contract of sale. Read those. Do not rely on a verbal assurance from the sales office.

### Should I wait to deposit the contract until the building is finished?

No. The six-month period runs from signature, and the point of the deposit is to fix your priority while the building is still at risk of being charged again.

### Can the developer promise me 5 per cent VAT?

The developer can apply the rate only if you qualify and the Tax Department accepts the application. Area, value, and use as a primary and permanent residence are your facts, not the project’s marketing.
