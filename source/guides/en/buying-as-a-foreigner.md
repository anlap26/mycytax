---
title: Buying property in Cyprus as a foreigner, step by step
description: Who can buy, the District Administration permit for non-EU buyers, the contract, the Land Registry deposit and the point at which title can pass.
---

A foreign buyer can acquire property in Cyprus, but the path is not the same for everyone. Citizens of an EU member state are not treated as “aliens” under the Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) Law, Cap. 109, and the Ministry of Interior’s purchasing-property page is aimed at citizens of non-EU states. EEA citizens are in the same practical position as EU citizens in the way Cap. 109 is applied. Everyone else, including a British buyer after Brexit, needs permission before the Land Registry will register the title in that person’s name.

## What a non-EU buyer may be allowed to buy

The Ministry of Interior states the current administrative limits in plain language. A foreign national, or a foreign couple, can seek permission for a plot or undivided land of up to 4,000 square metres, to build a dwelling for the owner to live in. Couples receive one joint permission. The alternative is up to two units, which may be at different stages of construction. Those two units can be two dwellings, or one dwelling and a shop of up to 100 square metres, or one dwelling and an office of up to 250 square metres. The ceiling applies to the couple together, not to each spouse on top of the other.

The application is form COMM 145, filed with the District Administration of the district where the property sits. The Ministry says no fee is charged and that a complete application is usually dealt with in two to three weeks. The checklist on that page still mentions a contract “with the requisite stamp duty”. Stamp duty was repealed for a document that no party had signed before 1 January 2026, so a 2026 contract should not need stamps. Take the current checklist from the District Administration rather than an old photocopy.

Permission is a condition of registration. You can sign a contract before it is granted. You should not assume the transfer will go through if the property is outside what the Ministry describes, for example a third dwelling, or a shop larger than the published limit, or a development plot bought for resale rather than a home. A company controlled by a non-EU person can itself be an “alien” for Cap. 109. Buying through a Cypriot company does not, by itself, remove the permission.

## Before you sign

Ask for a search certificate from the Department of Lands and Surveys. It shows who is registered as owner and which mortgages, memos and deposited contracts already bind the property. If the unit has no separate title yet, the search is of the mother plot, and you need to see the planning permit and the building permit, not only a brochure. Walk the unit against the floor plan that will be attached to the contract. A difference in area or in the parking space is much cheaper to resolve before signature than after.

Agree who pays the Land Registry transfer fee or the VAT, the connection deposits, and any common-expense arrears. On a resale those fees are usually the buyer’s. On a new home from a developer, VAT usually replaces the transfer fee, but only if the VAT Department’s certificate is produced at transfer. Neither point should be left as a verbal aside.

## The contract and the six-month deposit

The contract should name the parties, describe the property so that it can be identified, state the price and the payment dates, and be signed by everyone. Under the Sale of Immovable Property (Specific Performance) Law 81(I)/2011, a contract that meets those conditions can be deposited at the district lands office within six months of signature. The Department of Lands and Surveys tells buyers to deposit it, and says that a late deposit can increase the transfer fee by 10 per cent when title is eventually transferred. Deposit also creates a charge on the property, in order of time, and preserves the right to ask a court for an order that the property be registered in the buyer’s name if the seller does not transfer.

There is a filing fee. Guides that follow the Department’s forms put it at €50. Confirm the figure on the day, because a fee schedule can change without the law on the six-month period changing.

If the seller is a developer and the land is mortgaged, ask for a bank waiver that releases your unit as you pay. A deposited contract does not, by itself, remove a mortgage that was already registered. The search certificate is what shows the order.

## Money, VAT and the permit

Pay the price by a route your bank can explain. For an immigration permit under Regulation 6(2) the funds have to come from abroad. For an ordinary purchase there is no such statutory sentence, but Cypriot banks still ask for source-of-funds evidence under anti-money-laundering rules. Budget for that paperwork at the start.

If the property is a resale and VAT is not charged, budget for Land Registry transfer fees on the scale of 3, 5 and 8 per cent, generally halved. If VAT is charged on that same transfer, the transfer fee under Chapter 17 is not collected, provided the VAT certificate is filed. The 5 per cent VAT rate is only for a qualifying primary residence within the area and value limits. A holiday home or a buy-to-let is ordinarily 19 per cent. The calculators on this site apply those rules and link the sources.

File COMM 145 as soon as you have the contract, the title or planning papers, passports and evidence of funds. The Ministry’s list also asks for a marriage certificate where spouses do not share a surname. Do not wait until the developer says the title is ready. The permission has to exist before registration, and a refused or narrowed permission is a problem you want while the contract can still be revisited.

## After the keys

Title in your name is the end of the purchase, not the end of the administration. The municipality or the community board will want the property on its roll for local fees. Electricity and water are transferred into the buyer’s name against a deposit. If you will let the property, the rental-income page on this site covers income tax, the 20 per cent deduction and GHS. If you will live in it and you want the 5 per cent VAT, the Tax Department expects the home to be your primary and permanent residence, not a pied-à-terre, and it can reclaim the difference if that stops being true.

None of these steps is a substitute for a lawyer who reads the contract and the search certificate. The order above is the one the official pages support: know whether you need permission, check the title, sign a contract that can be deposited, deposit it, pay the tax that actually applies, and only then expect the Land Registry to register you.

## FAQ

### Can an EU citizen buy without the District Administration permit?

The Ministry of Interior’s permit page is addressed to citizens of non-EU states, including companies they control. EU citizens are outside Cap. 109’s definition of an alien. Buying through a company needs a separate look, because the company’s own status can bring Cap. 109 back.

### Does the permit have to come before I sign?

No. The Ministry treats the contract as one of the papers filed with COMM 145. Registration of title is what waits for the permission. Signing a contract for something the Ministry will not permit is still a bad idea, because you may be left with a personal claim rather than a property.

### Is there a fee for the acquisition permit?

The Ministry of Interior says no fee is payable for the permission itself. That is separate from Land Registry fees, VAT, and the fee for depositing the contract.
