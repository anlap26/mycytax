Mortgage: Cyprus, Germany, Greece and Spain

The European Central Bank’s average rate on new euro housing loans in September 2026, the Central Bank of Cyprus loan-to-value ceilings, and the purchase taxes each country actually publishes.

Figures on this site are taken from the official and primary sources linked on each page and describe the position as published for 2026. They are general information, not legal, tax or investment advice, and not a quote from the Land Registry or the Tax Department. Rules, reliefs and local fees change, and a transaction can fall outside the ordinary case. Check the linked source, and take advice on your own facts, before you rely on a result.

Last checked: November 2026.

Short answer

In September 2026 the annualised agreed rate on new euro loans to households for house purchase, all initial fixation periods together, was 3.26 per cent in Cyprus, 4.09 per cent in Germany, 3.24 per cent in Greece and 2.99 per cent in Spain. Those are the European Central Bank’s MFI interest-rate statistics, new business, not a bank’s offer and not a split between residents and non-residents. A repayment at a rate your own bank has offered is the mortgage calculator. That calculator does not publish a market rate. This page does, and it says which statistic it is.

The national central banks compile the national contributions. The number used here is the one the ECB publishes, so that the four countries are on one definition.

Loan to value

The Central Bank of Cyprus has published ceilings, notified to the European Systemic Risk Board. They are 80 per cent where the facility finances the borrower’s primary residence, and 70 per cent for other property. A further ceiling of 50 per cent applies to credit facilities to property-developing companies financing the acquisition or construction of property the notification defines as luxurious, above €5,000 per square metre. The ceilings bind credit institutions operating in Cyprus. They are not a resident-versus-non-resident table. A non-resident buying a house that is not their primary residence is in the 70 per cent category if the bank classifies it that way. A bank may lend less. The ceiling is not an offer.

For Germany, Greece and Spain, the ECB statistics and the central-bank pages checked for this article do not publish one official “typical loan-to-value for non-residents”. This page does not print 60 per cent, or 70 per cent, or 80 per cent, for those countries as if it were a rule. Ask the bank. In Cyprus, ask the bank as well, and then check the answer against the ceiling.

Cyprus, in the detail already used on this site. Transfer fees are on the Department of Lands and Surveys scale of 3, 5 and 8 per cent, and the legislation then exempts half, unless the case is one the half-exemption does not cover. Where VAT is charged and the certificate is produced, the transfer fee is not charged. Standard VAT is 19 per cent. The 5 per cent rate covers the first 130 square metres and up to €350,000 of a qualifying primary residence, if the whole area is not over 190 square metres and the whole value is not over €475,000. Stamp duty is nil where no party signed before 1 January 2026. The state immovable-property tax is not charged for years from 2017. Municipal refuse, property rates and sewerage are local, and this site does not invent a typical bill. Work the Cyprus side in the transfer-fee, VAT and stamp-duty calculators, and read the total-cost guide.

Germany. The real-estate transfer tax is a federal tax whose rate each Land sets. The German Notary Institute’s table of 28 January 2026 runs from 3.5 per cent in Bavaria to 6.5 per cent in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein. North Rhine-Westphalia’s own tax office states 6.5 per cent for contracts notarised from 1 January 2015. Confirm the Land before you exchange. A purchase of German land is notarised. Notary fees and land-register fees follow the federal fee schedules. They are not a round “2 per cent”, and this page will not call them one. From 1 January 2025 the annual real-property tax is charged under the reformed rules, and the municipal multiplier is local. The federal ministry does not publish one national bill.

Greece. The Independent Authority for Public Revenue says the buyer pays transfer tax at 3 per cent of the taxable value, and that a municipal contribution of 3 per cent is charged on that principal tax. The taxable value is not always the price in the contract. A first-home exemption exists for buyers the Authority lists, including Greek citizens and citizens of the European Union and the European Economic Area who meet the ownership test. The Authority’s headline ceilings include €200,000 for an unmarried buyer of a home and €250,000 for a married buyer, with further amounts for disability and children on the same page. A buyer outside those categories should not assume the exemption. ENFIA is charged each year on property in Greece on 1 January, on the E9 return, in up to ten monthly instalments, each not less than €10, the last by the last working day of February of the following year. This page does not print a typical ENFIA.

Spain. Article 91 of the VAT law applies 10 per cent to the supply of buildings, or parts of buildings, apt for use as dwellings, including up to two garage spaces and annexes transferred together. That is the state VAT. It is not the tax in the Canary Islands, Ceuta or Melilla, where other indirect taxes apply, and this page does not state those rates. A resale between private parties is charged to transfer tax, which is ceded to the autonomous communities, so there is no single national rate to print. The stamp duty on a notarial deed for a transaction that bears VAT is also set regionally. The annual tax on owning the property, IBI, is municipal. Notary and registry fees follow official tariffs. None of those is reduced to one percentage here.

Caveats before you compare a monthly payment

The September averages are not the rate a non-resident will be offered, and they are not the rate a resident will be offered. They are the average of what was agreed that month. Fees, an interest-only start, a foreign-currency income and a life policy change the payment the calculator does not model. Cyprus has a published ceiling on loan-to-value. The other three countries, on the sources checked in November 2026, do not publish a resident-versus-non-resident ceiling this page can quote. Purchase taxes are not interchangeable: a Cyprus resale, a Bavarian notarised purchase, a Greek transfer-tax return and a Spanish new-build VAT invoice are four different bills. Annual holding cost is local in all four, and only Greece publishes a national annual property tax, ENFIA, whose amount is still an assessment and not a rate you can multiply by the price.

Questions

Is 3.26 per cent the Cyprus mortgage rate?

It is the European Central Bank’s average annualised agreed rate on new euro housing loans in Cyprus in September 2026. It is not an offer. The rate in your loan agreement is the one the calculator should use.

Do non-residents get a lower loan-to-value in Cyprus?

The Central Bank’s ceilings are 80 per cent for a primary residence and 70 per cent for other property, plus 50 per cent for certain developer finance of property above €5,000 per square metre. They are not written as a passport test. A bank may lend less than the ceiling.

Why is there no single German or Spanish purchase-tax rate?

Germany’s transfer tax is set by each Land, between 3.5 and 6.5 per cent on the Notary Institute’s table of 28 January 2026. Spain’s transfer tax and its stamp duty on deeds are set by the autonomous communities. Printing one number would be wrong for most purchases.
