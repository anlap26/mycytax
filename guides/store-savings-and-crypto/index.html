How to safely store savings and crypto assets in 2026: review of hardware wallets and exchanges

How bank deposits, a hardware wallet and an exchange balance differ in 2026, including the Cyprus deposit scheme and what Ledger, Trezor, Binance and Bybit actually hold.

Saving and holding crypto are different jobs, and 2026 has not made them the same. A balance at a Cyprus bank is a claim on that bank, covered up to a published ceiling by the deposit-guarantee scheme. A coin left on an exchange is a claim on that company. A coin controlled by a hardware wallet is one you can move only if you still have the keys. This page separates those three. It does not tell you what share of your own money belongs in any of them.

Crypto can lose most or all of its price. An exchange can delay a withdrawal. A hardware wallet insures neither of those. Nothing below is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold an asset, or to move a sum you still need for a property transfer.

Cash and crypto fail in different ways

Price and custody are easy to mix up. The price of a coin can fall while the key is perfectly safe. The key can be lost while the price is rising. A bank deposit has a third problem, which is the bank itself, and a published scheme that pays eligible depositors if that bank cannot pay.

If you are counting money for a purchase in Cyprus, keep the part you will need for the price, the VAT and the Land Registry fee as cash you can send. The transfer-fee calculator, the VAT calculator and the total-cost guide exist so that figure is a number you have worked, not a guess you then lock behind a recovery phrase. Money you may have to send to a lawyer in the next few months is a poor candidate for a wallet.

Money you can leave for years may stay in the bank, go into listed investments, which the broker guide describes without picking a portfolio, or already be crypto. This page is about that last case.

What the Cyprus deposit scheme actually covers

The Central Bank of Cyprus describes the Deposit Guarantee and Resolution of Credit and Other Institutions Scheme. The maximum compensation is €100,000 per depositor, per credit institution, including accrued interest. Accounts you hold at the same institution are added together. The scheme covers deposits in any currency. It does not cover every balance that looks like savings. The Central Bank’s own list excludes, among other categories, deposits by banks, financial institutions, investment firms and insurance undertakings, deposits by some public authorities, and deposits whose holder was never identified.

There is a second rule that matters if you have just sold a home. The Central Bank’s FAQ treats certain deposits that come from a private residential property sale as a temporary high balance. The additional cover is up to €300,000, for no longer than 12 months, counted from the earlier of the day the money was credited and the day it became legally transferable. You have to be able to prove the source. In the Bank’s own example a joint account is split, and the extra cover is split with it. A sum the same bank can already claim from you, such as a loan instalment that has fallen due, can be set off against the deposit. Read the current FAQ before you treat either figure as cash you could collect in the week a bank failed. The wording can be amended, and what counts as proof is the scheme’s question, not this site’s.

A balance above the ordinary ceiling is not covered merely because it came from a flat. The extra cover is temporary, capped and conditional, and a brand is not always the legal entity on the account. Ask which institution is the member of the scheme. None of that cover reaches a hardware wallet, an exchange balance or a transfer app. The bank-account page is the note on opening the account itself.

What a hardware wallet does

A hardware wallet is a small device that holds the private keys and signs a transaction on the device, so the keys are not sitting on the laptop you use for email. The coins are not inside the device. They are entries on a public network. The device is the tool that proves you are allowed to move them.

The backup is the recovery phrase, often twelve or twenty-four words, shown once when you set the device up. Anyone who has the phrase has the keys. Anyone who loses the phrase and loses the device loses access. The manufacturer cannot look you up and restore it. A photo of the phrase, a copy in a cloud note, or a version typed into a website removes the point of the device. An extra passphrase on top of the words is a real second lock. It is also a real way to lock yourself out. Use one only after you have written down how you will recover it, and after you have tested that you can.

Buy the device from the maker. A marketplace listing is a common place to find a unit that was already initialised, with a phrase someone else kept. If a seller includes a card with words already written, that card is not a backup. It is someone else’s key. Generate the phrase yourself, on the device, and write it where you will still find it in five years. Then send a small amount, restore once, and confirm the coins come back before you move a balance you would hate to lose.

Ledger and Trezor, without a ranking

Ledger and Trezor are the two names that come up because both companies have sold hardware wallets for years and both still sell them directly. This page does not rank them and does not print a price. The current model, the supported assets and the price are on the maker’s own site, and they change. Compare those pages on the day, not a screenshot from a forum.

The difference you can actually check is how much of the device the maker lets you inspect. Trezor, from SatoshiLabs, publishes the software that runs on its devices and says that code can be reviewed. Ledger’s devices use a secure element. Ledger’s own technical description says the operating system on that element is closed, under the chip vendor’s restrictions, while much of the application code is published. A closed element is a design choice. Open code is not a promise that no bug will be found. What you can insist on is that the maker tells you which of those you are buying.

Whichever name you pick, the phrase stays offline. Do not type it into the companion app, and do not type it into any page that offers to “verify” or “sync” the wallet. The genuine setup shows the words on the device. A page that asks you to enter them is the attack, whether or not you are on a VPN. The note on working online covers the network. It does not cover a phrase you typed into the wrong form.

What you leave on Binance or Bybit

Binance and Bybit are custodial exchanges. You can buy, sell and convert there. The keys to a balance you leave on the exchange belong to the company, not to you. That is a reasonable place for a trade you are about to do. It is a poor vault for an amount you have no plan to move this month. The company can ask for more documents, delay a withdrawal, or restrict an account while it reviews one. Those are ordinary operating facts. They are not a prediction that either firm will fail. They are the reason a long-term holding does not have to sit there.

The company you contract with is the legal entity named when you open the account. This page will not guess that entity from a Cyprus address. Read the terms for the legal name and for any regulator they name. An exchange balance is not a deposit at a Cyprus credit institution, so the €100,000 ceiling above does not apply to it. Rules that compensate investors in securities are a different scheme, and they do not turn a coin balance into a guaranteed deposit.

When a withdrawal does land in a Cyprus bank, the bank may ask where it came from. Keep the records of what you bought and what you withdrew before anyone asks. A hardware wallet does not create that paper for you.

What the money is for comes first

Cash for completion, for a tax bill, or for several months of living costs belongs where you can send it without a recovery phrase. The capital-gains calculator shows the tax on one property sale. It does not tell you to convert what remains. The rental-yield calculator shows tax on rent. Rent you are about to spend is not a crypto position.

Tax is not settled by the brand on the box. Whether a disposal is taxable depends on your residence, your domicile and what you actually did. The tax-residency guide and the non-dom guide cover the status questions this site does answer. They do not publish a crypto rate. This page will not invent one. Ask the Tax Department, or an adviser who will sign the answer.

From 1 January 2026 the rate on a disposal that falls in Article 20E is on the crypto tax page, taken from the Tax Department’s questions and from the statute on CyLaw. This storage page still does not decide whether your own disposal falls in that article.

Questions

Is a hardware wallet a bank deposit?

No. An eligible deposit at a Cyprus credit institution can fall under the deposit-guarantee scheme, up to the ceiling the Central Bank publishes. Coins controlled by a hardware wallet are not that deposit. The device only holds the keys.

Does the €100,000 scheme cover Binance or Bybit?

No. The scheme covers eligible deposits at a member credit institution. A balance on an exchange is a claim on the exchange. Read the company named in the terms you accepted.

What if I lose the recovery phrase?

If the device is gone as well, the coins are gone. The maker never stored the phrase and cannot restore it. Write it down, and test a restore while the amount is still small.

Should I buy the device from a marketplace?

Buy it from the maker. A phrase a seller has already written down is a key someone else may still have. Generate the words on the device yourself.

Does the brand of wallet decide the tax?

No. Residence, domicile and what you did decide whether a disposal is taxable. This page does not give a crypto tax rate.
