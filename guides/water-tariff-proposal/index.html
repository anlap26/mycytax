A single Cyprus water tariff, before the cabinet on 17 November 2026

On 17 November 2026 the interior ministry asked the cabinet to approve one water scale for the whole island, including a charge on new development. The reports that day said the texts were not yet law.

On 17 November 2026 the Interior Ministry asked the cabinet to approve one set of water and sewerage charges for the whole of Cyprus, and to authorise the minister to send the texts to the House of Representatives. Philenews, reporting that morning, named Interior Minister Constantinos Ioannou as the minister making the request. The same report said the Law Office had already read the regulations. Until the cabinet approves the texts and the House votes, this scale is not the charge on a meter.

Why one scale was drafted

Before the district organisations of local government, water and sewerage were billed by water boards, sewerage boards, municipalities and community councils. The charge depended on the place. Philenews says the district organisations, the Ministry of Agriculture, Rural Development and Environment, and the Water Development Department then agreed a single framework. The package also sets common caps on sewerage and stormwater fees. The report does not print those caps as amounts in euro.

What a household would pay

Philenews sets out a monthly standing charge of €4, then a rising price per cubic metre.

Monthly useProposed price per cubic metre
1–10€1.05
11–20€1.45
21–30€1.80
31–40€2.50
41–60€3
61–80€5
Beyond 81€10

The paper's phrase for the top band is "beyond 81 cubic metres". It does not give a separate price for the 81st cubic metre on its own. On the bands it does print, 10 cubic metres would be the €4 standing charge plus 10 times €1.05, which is €14.50, before sewerage or any other fee. That is arithmetic from the printed scale, not a bill the ministry has issued.

Who would pay less

Vulnerable households would keep the €4 standing charge and pay €1 per cubic metre up to 10, €1.20 from 11 to 20, €1.40 from 21 to 40, €1.60 from 41 to 60, and €2 beyond 61. Philenews lists the groups: people on the guaranteed minimum income or public assistance; people receiving the severe motor disability allowance or the special allowance for blind persons; low-income pensioners aged 70 or over; people with paraplegia or quadriplegia; and large families or five-member families who receive child benefit.

More than 100 mountain and isolated villages in Nicosia, Larnaca, Limassol and Paphos, supplied by government water works, would be able to get a reduction of up to 50 per cent on the standard consumption charge. Philenews names the villages. This page does not copy that list.

Akrotiri, in the Kourion municipal district, would have a transition. From publication of the regulations until 30 June 2029, the first ten cubic metres would be €0.20, plus the €4 standing charge. From 1 July 2029 to 30 June 2031 that rate would be €0.90. After that, the countrywide scale.

Connection, deposits and other users

Philenews also prints fixed fees: a connection at €25; installation capped at €700; annual maintenance capped at €30, charged in proportion if the premises are supplied for only part of the year; a meter test or re-inspection at €50; reconnection at €35 in ordinary hours and €70 outside them; disconnection at €35.

Monthly deposits in the same report: €25 for a household and for a vulnerable household; €100 for communal areas, public and community sports grounds, green spaces and livestock; €200 for commercial and industrial users; €10,000 for a marina supplying leisure craft.

Commercial and industrial users would pay a standing charge of €35 a month, and a rate from €1.20 per cubic metre for the first 100 cubic metres to €1.50 beyond 1,501. Hotels would pay a standing charge of €200 a month, from €1.55 for the first 100 cubic metres to €2.40 beyond 501. The report does not print every step between those ends. Public and community sports grounds, green spaces and livestock farms would pay €4 a month and a flat €1.35 per cubic metre.

A charge on new development

For new development, Philenews says the fee on the land would be 1.2 per cent of the property's general valuation, plus a fee on the building area.

Building areaProposed fee per square metre
Up to 200 m²€1
201–450 m²€2
451–1,950 m²€3
Above 1,951 m²€4

That fee is not VAT on a new home, and it is not the Land Registry transfer fee. Philenews describes it inside a water and sewerage package. The report does not say that it replaces either tax. A purchase can meet more than one charge. This page does not add them into one total. The water texts were not in force in the reports of 17 November, and a general valuation is not a sale price.

The taxes that are already in force are on the tax overview. The VAT calculator and the transfer-fee calculator use those rules. Stamp duty is nil on a contract that nobody signed before 1 January 2026. A mortgage payment is a loan figure, not a water charge.

What still has to happen

Philenews says the Attorney General wanted a firmer statutory basis for a rule that lets a district organisation's board delegate powers to its director general and then to other staff. A bill, the District Local Government Organisations (Amendment) Law of 2026, was drafted beside the regulations. Dialogos, later on 17 November, still described the special rates as part of a package that needs the legislative process and a final vote in the House.

The charge on a bill today is the one the supplier already applies. The utilities page says how a supply agreement is opened. It does not treat this proposal as the tariff.

Questions

Will the next water bill use the €10 rate?

Not on the strength of the reports of 17 November 2026. They describe a proposal that still needed the cabinet and the House. The rate on a bill is the one the supplier charges today.

Is the 1.2 per cent the same thing as VAT?

No. Philenews describes it as a land fee on the general valuation, inside a water and sewerage package. It does not say that this fee replaces VAT. The VAT calculator on this site uses the Tax Department's rules, not this proposal.

Does the top rate start at 80 or at 81 cubic metres?

Philenews says €5 from the 61st to the 80th cubic metre and €10 beyond 81. It does not state a separate price for the 81st cubic metre alone.
