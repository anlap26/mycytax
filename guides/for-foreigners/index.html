Cyprus tax for foreigners in 2026

Moving, non-dom status, double-tax treaties, the digital nomad visa, a company at 15%, buying property, crypto and a comparison with other countries.

Last checked: 26 November 2026.

Short answer

A foreigner in Cyprus meets several statutes, not one tax. Tax residence is counted in days. Non-dom status takes Special Defence Contribution off dividends and interest. It does not take off income tax. From 2026 a company pays corporate tax at 15%. A home is either VAT or transfer fees, and stamp duty on a new contract is nil. A gain on disposing of a crypto-asset that was not mined is, from 1 January 2026, taxed at 8% under Article 20E.

What the 2026 reform changed

On its company-return page the Tax Department publishes a table: corporate tax from 2026 is 15%. The same table puts 12.5% on 2013–2025.

Business in Cyprus, a government site, publishes the income-tax bands from the 2026 tax year: 0% up to €22,000, 20% from €22,001 to €32,000, 25% to €42,000, 30% to €72,000 and 35% above that.

The Department’s defence-contribution page, from 2026, puts dividends at 5%, interest of individuals at 17% (3% where total income is under €12,000), abolishes the charge on rent, and abolishes deemed dividend distribution on profits from 2026. A dividend from a Cyprus company out of profits up to the 2025 tax year, received by 31 December 2031, stays at 17% in the Department’s own note.

A person who is not domiciled remains outside the contribution on dividends and interest. The €250,000 extension is not an entrance fee. The non-dom guide describes it.

Article 20E of the Income Tax Law, in the CyLaw consolidation, taxes profits from disposing of crypto-assets at 8%. The Department’s questions and answers say mining is taxed at the ordinary rates, not at 8%.

Law 239(I)/2025 repealed stamp duty on a document a party signs on or after 1 January 2026.

What this map does not guess

Treaty withholding percentages are not printed here. They have to be read in the text the Ministry of Finance links. A Russian suspension of part of the treaty is not recorded on the Ministry’s list page, and this page does not treat it as a fact. Malta’s 2026 personal bands were not on the rates page that was reviewed. The middle bands of the Greek 2026 scale depend on the number of children, and the zero-child column did not read reliably out of the page text, so the calculator does not apply it. Social-insurance rates and a dirham-to-euro rate are not filled in.

Questions

Does non-dom lower the income tax on a salary?

No. The 2026 bands are the same. The status removes Special Defence Contribution on dividends and interest. The calculator shows both columns on the same figures.

Is corporate tax still 12.5%?

On the Tax Department’s company-return page the row “from 2026” is 15%. The same table puts 12.5% on 2013–2025.

Is there a treaty with Israel?

Israel is not on the Ministry of Finance list read on 26 November 2026. CRS information exchange is a different instrument, not a double-tax treaty.

Can the calculator be treated as the Department’s assessment?

No. It illustrates published rates. Social insurance, GHS, deductions and special regimes are outside it unless the page says they are in.
