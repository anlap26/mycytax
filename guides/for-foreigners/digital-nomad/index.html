The Cyprus digital nomad visa

The Migration Department permit: at least €3,500 a month net, a ceiling of 500 permits, one year with a renewal. It is not a tax exemption.

Short answer

The Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa scheme is a temporary permit for a national of a country outside the EU and outside the EEA who works remotely for an employer abroad or for clients abroad. The Migration Department is accepting applications again. The ceiling is 500 permits. The net monthly income is at least €3,500 after contributions and tax. The permit does not exempt income tax and it does not create non-dom status.

Who the Department describes

The Department’s page, posted on 31 December 2025, says the applicant can work through telecommunications, is not tied to a place, works for a company registered abroad or is self-employed for clients abroad, and shows stable and sufficient net income of at least €3,500 a month after contributions and taxes are deducted.

The Department’s document list, form MVIS dated 19 March 2025, adds 20% for a spouse or partner and 15% for each child. Income is shown by salary payment and six months of bank statements. Where the net income is not visible from the papers, the Department asks for a written confirmation that the net income is not below the threshold.

The work must not be work for an employer registered in Cyprus, and it must not be a service to a client established in Cyprus. That is what the same list says.

Length, and the family

The first permit is for one year. A renewal is for no more than two years. Family members may stay for the same period and may not work or carry on an economic activity in Cyprus.

The application is filed at the Department’s central office in Nicosia within three months of arrival, in person or through a representative. Documents are translated and certified as the list says. If the person is already lawfully in Cyprus under another status, the Department allows the application to be filed there.

The Council of Ministers introduced the scheme by a decision of 15 October 2021 with a ceiling of 100 permits, and on 3 March 2022 raised the ceiling to 500. The Department’s page says the ceiling remains 500.

The tax sentence on the scheme itself

The scheme page says that if a person stays in the Republic for one or more periods exceeding 183 days in total in the same tax year, they are considered a Cyprus tax resident, provided they are not a tax resident of any other country.

The Tax Department’s page on the 183-day rule does not repeat that extra proviso. It counts days. The 60-day rule is separate, with its own conditions, including not spending more than 183 days in another country. Both texts have to be read. The scheme does not replace the day count.

The income-tax bands from 2026, if the income falls in them, are on that same relocation page. The scheme does not issue non-dom status.

Other residence routes, including MEU1 for a Union citizen and Regulation 6(2), are not replaced by this visa. The map is the residence options page.

Questions

Is €3,500 before tax or after?

The Department’s page says net, after contributions and taxes are deducted. It is not salary before withholdings.

Can the visa be used to work for a Cyprus company?

The document list requires that the work does not concern an employer registered in Cyprus, or a client established in Cyprus.

Does the visa make someone non-dom?

No. The permit is an immigration document. Domicile is described separately by the Tax Department.

Does an EU citizen use this scheme?

The Department’s page limits the scheme to nationals of countries outside the EU and the EEA. A Union citizen uses the MEU1 certificate.
