Residence options: EU citizens and third-country nationals

A Union citizen uses the MEU1 registration certificate. A third-country visitor uses the MVIS8 permit and may not work. An investment permit under Regulation 6(2) is a third route, with its own money tests.

Short answer

The route depends on the passport, and then on what you will do. A citizen of the European Union, the European Economic Area or Switzerland who stays longer than three months applies for a registration certificate, form MEU1, within four months of entry. The fee is €20. The certificate does not expire. The person may work. A third-country national who is visiting, and not working, applies for a temporary residence permit, form MVIS8, before the visa expires. The fee is €70, plus €70 if this is the first registration in the Aliens’ Register. The holder may not carry out an economic activity. A third-country national who will invest on the Department’s current criteria uses Regulation 6(2), which is neither of those forms.

The full steps, the grounds and the fees are on the yellow slip and pink slip page. This page is the map, so the three files are not mixed.

Union citizens

Up to three months, a valid identity card or passport is enough. Longer than that, the person is a worker or self-employed, or has sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance. Family members who are themselves Union citizens apply with the same certificate. Family members who are not Union citizens are a different card, MEU2. The Ministry’s residence-card page is the place for that distinction. After five years of legal residence a permanent card, MEU3, can be issued for €20 if the requirements are met. None of this is tax residence. Count the days separately.

Third-country nationals who are not investing

The visitor categories are on the Migration Department’s page: an autonomous visitor, a dependent spouse, a dependent minor child, and the narrower categories the page names. The first permit is generally one year and is renewable if the criteria still hold. It is not a work permit. A person who will be employed is in the residence-and-employment procedure, which this page does not restate, because the fees and the office are not the visitor page’s. Read the Department’s page for that category before you use MVIS8.

Third-country nationals who invest

Regulation 6(2), on the criteria in force from 2 May 2023, is an immigration permit tied to an investment of at least €300,000 in one of the categories the Department lists, plus a secured annual income of at least €50,000, with the uplifts for a spouse and children. It is not citizenship and not tax residence. The guide has the categories, the income rule for a flat, and what the permit does not allow. A non-EU buyer of land also needs the Ministry of Interior’s acquisition permission before title is registered. That permission is not the residence permit. The buying guide keeps them apart.

What to do with the answer

Pick one route and follow its form. A yellow-slip checklist will not get a pink slip issued, and a Regulation 6(2) file will not be examined as a visitor application. When the permit or the certificate exists, GESY can use the migration registry to identify a non-Cypriot. Until then, the insurance the Department asked for is the cover you hold.

Questions

Can an EU citizen use the pink slip?

No. The pink slip is the visitor permit for a third-country national. A Union citizen uses MEU1.

Does Regulation 6(2) let me work?

The Department’s criteria say no, except as a director of a company you invested in under that policy. The guide states the limit.

Which page has the documents?

The yellow-slip and pink-slip page links the Department’s own lists. This overview does not copy them.
