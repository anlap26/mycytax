Protecting banking and financial operations online: why you need a reliable VPN when working from abroad

What a VPN changes and does not change when you bank or work from abroad, and how NordVPN, Surfshark and Proton VPN differ. A VPN does not change tax residency.

A VPN encrypts the path between your device and a server the VPN company runs. On a café, hotel or airport network, that stops the person who runs the router from reading the pages you open. It does not make a bank transfer safe by itself, it does not hide you from your bank, and it does not change where you are tax resident. This page is about that narrower job, for people who bank or work from Cyprus and from elsewhere, and about three providers readers ask for by name: NordVPN, Surfshark and Proton VPN.

Nothing here says you must buy one. A careful login on a network you trust can be enough. A VPN on a network you do not trust is a reasonable tool. It is not a financial product, and it is not advice about your taxes.

What the tunnel actually changes

Without a VPN, the local network can see which sites you connect to and, on a plain page, the contents. A bank’s own site is normally encrypted already. The VPN adds a second encrypted hop as far as the VPN server, so the café sees a connection to the VPN rather than a list of the banks and brokers you opened. From the VPN server onward, the traffic goes out to those sites as usual. The bank sees a login from the VPN’s address, not from the café. The bank still sees that it is you, because you typed the password.

That is the whole mechanical gain. It matters on networks you do not control: a hotel, a coworking space, a ferry, a rented flat whose router you did not set up. It matters less on a home connection in your own name, where the interesting risks are phishing and a stolen password, not the landlord’s router. Use the bank’s own app when the bank offers one. Check the address yourself. A VPN will happily encrypt your visit to a lookalike site.

Turn the VPN on before you join the open network if you have decided to use one. A kill switch, where the app has one, drops the connection if the tunnel drops, so traffic does not leak onto the café network in the gap. Confirm the switch exists in the app you installed, not in a review. Features get renamed and moved.

What it does not change

It does not authenticate the bank. Phishing still works if you type the password into the wrong page. It does not store your passwords, and it does not replace the second factor the bank sends you. It does not protect a wallet recovery phrase. Typing that phrase into any website is the failure described in the storage guide, VPN or not.

It does not decide tax residency. The tax-residency guide is about days and ties. A server in another country is neither. Using a VPN to appear as if you live somewhere you do not is a bad idea twice. A bank that required you to be in a country may treat it as a breach of its terms. A tax authority will not treat the IP address as your residence, in either direction. Do not buy a VPN in order to look resident, or non-resident, of Cyprus.

It does not make a transfer cheaper, and it does not hold your money. The broker guide is where a surplus and a currency transfer are discussed. Logging in to a broker through a tunnel does not change the account, the fee or the tax.

Banks differ on foreign addresses. Some lock an account when the login appears in a country you have never used. Some lock it when the address belongs to a VPN range they already know. Some forbid a VPN in the terms. Read the terms of the bank you actually use, including a home-country bank you still open from Cyprus. If a login is blocked, the remedy is the bank’s own support, not a second VPN provider. The bank-account page is the local note on opening an account here. It is not a promise that any given bank will accept a tunnel.

NordVPN, Surfshark and Proton VPN

The three names are not three identical companies, and two of them are not independent of each other.

NordVPN and Surfshark are separate apps. In 2022 the companies announced that Surfshark was joining Nord Security in one group, and they said the products would keep operating separately, with separate infrastructure. Choosing both does not give you two owners. It gives you two products from one group. That can still be a reasonable choice if you prefer one app’s behaviour. It is not a backup plan against the group itself.

Proton VPN is a different owner. Proton says the service is provided by Proton AG in Switzerland, and that the main shareholder is the Proton Foundation, a Swiss non-profit. Proton also runs an encrypted email service. The jurisdiction and the owner are the points that distinguish it from the other two on this page. They are not a score, and this page does not rank the three.

What you can compare without a ranking is public and checkable. Read which company the terms name, and in which country that company sits. Read the no-logs statement the provider actually publishes, and any independent audit they link, including the date on it. Confirm a kill switch in the app. Ignore a user-count or a “fastest” claim. Those numbers move, and this page will not repeat one it cannot keep current. Prices move too. The provider’s own page on the day is the price. A discount on a partner button is still their price, and it can differ from another route.

Working from abroad, in practice

People in the audience of this site are often in one of two places. They live in Cyprus and still open a bank or a broker elsewhere. Or they are outside Cyprus for a stretch and still open a Cyprus bank. In both cases the network you are on is the question, not a flag on the account.

Prefer a connection you control, such as a phone hotspot you set up yourself, over a hotel network you did not. If you use the hotel network, the VPN is the tool that hides the session from that network. It does not hide it from the bank. Tell the bank before a long trip if their own app asks you to, so a foreign login is less of a surprise. That phone call is worth more than a second subscription.

Keep the bank’s app updated, and do not install a “helper” that offers to log in for you. Do not type a seed phrase, a full card number or a one-time code into a page that arrived by email. None of that becomes safe because the little VPN icon is on.

The first months after a move have enough real admin without a false sense that a tunnel has handled it. The first-90-days guide and the resident handbook are the practical lists. A VPN is not on the list of registrations. It is a setting on the devices you already carry.

What this page will not claim

It will not call one of the three the safest, and it will not print a price, a server count or a speed. It will not say a VPN is required to bank from Cyprus. It will not say a VPN keeps you anonymous from a site you log in to. The site you log in to knows the account. The gain is limited, and it is real: the local network learns less, if the tunnel is up before you start.

Questions

Does a VPN make online banking safe?

It encrypts the path across a network you do not control. It does not stop phishing, and it does not replace the bank’s login and second factor.

Can a VPN change my tax residency?

No. Residency is about days and ties, not the country of a server. Do not use a VPN to look as if you live somewhere you do not.

Are NordVPN and Surfshark different companies?

They are separate apps. Since the 2022 announcement they have been part of one group, Nord Security, which said the products would keep separate infrastructure. Proton VPN is a different owner, Proton AG in Switzerland.

Will my bank allow a VPN?

Some banks lock a login from an unexpected country, and some lock addresses they know as VPN ranges. Read the terms of the bank you use. If a login is blocked, use the bank’s own support.

Do I need a VPN on my home connection in Cyprus?

Not for the reason this page describes. The gain is on a network you do not control. At home the larger risks are a fake page and a stolen password.
