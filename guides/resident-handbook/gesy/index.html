How to register for GESY and choose a personal doctor

The Health Insurance Organisation says healthcare under GESY needs two registrations: the beneficiary registry, and a personal doctor’s list.

Short answer

GESY, the General Healthcare System, is run by the Health Insurance Organisation. The Organisation says that access to its healthcare services needs two things. You enrol in the beneficiary registry, and you register on a personal doctor’s list. Doing only the first does not put you on a doctor’s list.

To enrol, you must already be in the Civil Registry, or in the Migration Department’s registry, and/or with the Social Insurance Services. The Organisation may ask for further evidence under the GHS Law of 2017. For a person who is not a Cypriot, a GESY enrolment request requires registration with the Civil Registry and Migration Department and a valid residence permit, apart from the narrow paper-form categories the Organisation lists.

This page does not state a contribution rate. The Organisation’s enrolment page describes the registration. It does not set out the percentages. The Tax Department’s rental page, which this site already uses, states GHS at 2.65 per cent of gross rent for an individual owner. Other categories have their own rates. Read the current HIO table rather than a figure repeated from memory. The rental calculator applies the 2.65 per cent figure and says what it does not model.

Steps

  1. Create an account on the HIO website and activate it. Creation is not activation. The beneficiary registration screen is at the GHS registration page.
  2. Sign in to the Beneficiary Portal and complete the enrolment. You fill the fields that identify you in the Civil Registry, the Migration registry or the Social Insurance records, plus an address and a telephone number.
  3. If the system cannot match you, it asks for an online registration request, which you print and post to the Health Insurance Organisation with the certificates it names. Until those papers arrive, that route is not finished.
  4. If you have no internet access, a person who already has a portal account may enrol you with your consent. Or you may visit a personal doctor, who can complete the beneficiary application. On that visit the doctor may also put you on the doctor’s list, which is the second step.
  5. Choose a personal doctor who is contracted with the Organisation. Search on the HIO website, in the Beneficiary Portal, or in the printed directories the Organisation says it makes available.
  6. Register on that doctor’s list, either by an online request or by visiting the practice. If the doctor accepts an online request, you are told by email or in the portal, and you still visit. At the visit the doctor confirms your details and you both sign the Form of Mutual Acceptance, which the doctor sends to the Organisation.

A paper enrolment form exists only for the categories the Organisation names: certain people exempt from migration formalities, including S1 holders who are diplomats or embassy staff and their families working in EU embassies, persons with “Native” status, and domestic staff in embassies, and two data-correction cases where a residence permit exists but the portal cannot take the name or the nationality. Everyone else uses the portal route. The paper form is not a general alternative.

Which doctor

Beneficiaries under 15 register with a personal doctor for children. From age 15 until 18, either a children’s doctor or an adult doctor. Over 18, an adult doctor. Over 65, an adult doctor or a doctor specialised in geriatrics.

A beneficiary over the age of 2 may change personal doctor once six months have passed since registration on that list. Up to the age of 2, the child may change whenever the parents wish.

Documents

Online enrolment uses the data that identify you in a registry the Organisation already holds, so the practical documents are the ones that put you in that registry first: a yellow slip or pink slip or another valid residence permit for a non-Cypriot, and, where the route is employment, the social insurance record. If the portal asks you to post certificates, the screen names them. This page does not add a private list.

What registration is not

It is not automatic on the day you land. It is not a substitute for the private insurance a pink-slip or Regulation 6(2) file may still require until you are actually inside GESY. The immigration-permit guide says health insurance continues if the family is not in GESY.

Questions

Is enrolment enough to see a GESY doctor?

No. The Organisation says you must be in the beneficiary registry and on a personal doctor’s list. The second step is a request the doctor accepts, or a visit at which the doctor adds you and you sign the Form of Mutual Acceptance.

Can I change doctor immediately?

Over the age of 2, only after six months from registration on that list. Up to the age of 2, whenever you wish. Both rules are the Organisation’s.

Does GESY replace a pink-slip insurance policy on day one?

Not by itself. Enrolment depends on the registries, and for a non-Cypriot on a valid residence permit. Until you are enrolled and on a doctor’s list, the insurance the Migration Department asked for is still the cover you hold.
