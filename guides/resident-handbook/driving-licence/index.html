Exchanging a foreign driving licence in Cyprus

An EU licence may be exchanged after six months, if you choose. A licence from a country the Road Transport Department treats as equivalent must be exchanged after six months. The application is form TOM 7D, in person.

Short answer

The Road Transport Department converts a foreign licence into a Cypriot one when the licence is from the European Union or from a country the Commissioner has notified as equivalent. EU holders may exchange after a six-month stay if they wish. Holders from a notified country are obliged to exchange after a six-month stay. The Department’s page says the applicant must have normal residence, which it describes as at least 185 days in Cyprus. A non-EU applicant also presents the alien registration certificate and the residence permit. A student presents a certificate of attendance at a higher-education institution for at least six months.

A separate road-safety leaflet on Gov.cy, written for visitors, says European citizens may drive on their own country’s licence, and names a list of non-European countries whose licences may be used for up to six months, after which a Cypriot licence is required without a driving test: Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Australia, Russia, Georgia, Ukraine, Serbia, Switzerland, Zimbabwe, the United States, Japan, Canada, New Zealand, South Africa and South Korea. The same leaflet says other non-European visitors may drive for up to 30 days on their national licence, or on a valid international permit. Read the leaflet and the Department’s conversion page together. The conversion page is the one that takes the application.

Steps

  1. Wait until the residence test on the Department’s page is met: at least 185 days, and the permit papers if you are not an EU citizen.
  2. Complete form TOM 7D, application for a driving licence.
  3. Take a recent photograph, 45 by 35 millimetres.
  4. Take your identity card, if you are a Cypriot returnee, or your alien registration certificate if you are not, in original and copy. Take your passport, original and a copy of the details page.
  5. Take the foreign licence, original and a copy of every page. The Department sends it to the authority that issued it.
  6. If the licence is not in English or Greek, take a translation by the Press and Information Office through an authorised translator, original and one copy.
  7. Take evidence of normal residence. The Department points to its own instructions for what counts.
  8. Appear in person at the district driver-examiner office. The Department says the file is checked there, and that you go to the district office of the Department for the issue of the licence.
  9. If you are 70 or older, or the categories are C, C+E, D, D+E, C1, C1+E, D1 or D1+E, take the medical fitness certificate the page requires.

Documents

The list above is the Department’s list, not a private one. This page does not state the fee. The Department’s conversion page, as read for this article, sets out the papers and the residence test and does not print a euro amount. Pay the fee the office charges on the day. Do not budget a number this page invented.

What the exchange is not

It is not the registration of a car. That is the vehicle page. It is not a test, for the countries the leaflet and the notification cover. A licence from a country that is neither in the European Union nor on the Commissioner’s notification is not converted on this page’s wording. Ask the Department before you assume a test is waived.

Questions

Must an EU citizen exchange?

The Road Transport Department says an EU holder may exchange after six months if they wish. The obligation it states is for holders from countries on the Commissioner’s notification. A visitor leaflet says European citizens may drive on their own licence. Read both, and do not treat a wish as a deadline.

How long is normal residence?

The Department’s conversion page says a foreign applicant must reside in Cyprus for at least 185 days, and a non-EU citizen must also present the alien registration certificate and the residence permit.

Do I bring photographs?

Yes. The Department asks for a recent photograph of 45 by 35 millimetres, which is a different rule from the Migration Department’s MEU1 counter, where the photograph is captured for you.
