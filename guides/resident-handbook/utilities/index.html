Electricity and water when you move into a Cyprus home

Electricity is the Electricity Authority of Cyprus. Water is the board or the community of the town, not one national supplier. Deposits exist. This page does not print a typical deposit.

Short answer

Electricity supply to a home, once the property is already connected, is an agreement with the Electricity Authority of Cyprus. A brand-new connection starts with the Authority’s distribution side, not with the supply agreement. Water is not national. Nicosia and Limassol publish their own application forms. Other towns have their own boards or community councils. A single “Cyprus water deposit” is not a figure any of those pages states for the whole island, so this page does not invent one.

Electricity, if the supply is already there

The Authority’s supply page says you sign an electricity supply agreement for a new connection, a reconnection, or a transfer when the tenant or the name changes. You can send the papers by email to the customer service centre, or you can visit. For a natural person the page asks for:

  • a title deed, a contract of sale, or a valid rental agreement, and if the title is in shares the applicant must hold at least half, or else a consent of the co-owners who together hold at least half;
  • an identity card, a passport, an alien registration certificate, or another legally valid identity document;
  • a power of attorney if someone else signs, certified, and the Authority says it accepts an authorisation of up to five years;
  • a deposit, paid by a tenant when the agreement is signed, while an owner is charged on the first bill.

A transfer also needs the existing customer’s consent to end the old agreement. The new customer opens a new account. The old one closes.

Electricity, if there is no connection yet

The distribution page says the network user completes the services-provision form. The form the page links is in Greek. You deliver it to a customer centre, or you email the district address the page lists: Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca and Famagusta, or Paphos. A small load and a large load follow different internal paths. After the terms are accepted and paid, a licensed electrical contractor submits plans for inspection. The supply agreement with the supply side is signed in the window the supply page describes, one to five days before the inspection date, once a date exists. This page does not state the connection charge. The supply page says connection charges are set by distribution and appear on the next bill.

Water

Ask the supplier that actually serves the property. The Nicosia water board publishes consumer requests, including a new meter. The Limassol board publishes connection and account-transfer forms, including an application for an account transfer by an individual. A village may be the community council rather than a city board. Take the same class of papers the electricity transfer uses, because a supplier needs to know who may put the account in their name: identity, and title, contract or lease. The exact list is the form of that supplier. Do not send Nicosia’s form to Limassol.

Documents

Identity. The right to occupy: title, contract or lease. For electricity, the Authority’s own list above. For water, the local form. A residence card is the identity document an alien registration certificate represents, where the electricity page names that certificate. It is not a substitute for the title or the lease.

Questions

Is there one water authority?

No. Nicosia and Limassol publish separate forms, and a community may be the supplier outside those boards. Use the supplier for the property.

When does a tenant pay the electricity deposit?

The Electricity Authority says a tenant pays the deposit when the supply agreement is signed. An owner is charged on the first bill. The page does not state the amount, and neither does this one.

Can I put the account in my name with only a yellow slip?

The electricity page asks for identity and for title, a contract of sale or a valid rental agreement. A residence card can be the identity document. It is not the document that shows you may occupy the property.
